EP06 // HISTORIC SITREP

March 4–5, 1804 (Castle Hill) & January 26, 1808 (Rum Rebellion)

Philip Cunningham & William Johnston (Castle Hill); Governor William Bligh (1808)
Authorities/Settlers: 0 fatal military casualties across both clashes  |  Resistants: ~15 convicts killed in battle at Rouse Hill; 9 executed; 2 hanged in chains; Bligh placed under house arrest for over a year
Major George Johnston & John Macarthur (NSW Corps cartel)
Crown intervention ending private military trade monopolies; deployment of Macquarie in 1810 to establish institutional governance.
1. TRIGGER
The suppression of the 1798 Irish Rebellion transporting United Irishmen to Sydney, intersecting with the NSW Corps monopoly over spirits and land barter.
2. FORCES
Agrarian Irish republican insurgency and martial law vs. entrenched military mercantilism and private syndicate cartels under John Macarthur.
3. COSTS
15 convicts executed at Castle Hill/Sydney; total subversion of Crown civil administration in the only military coup d etat on Australian soil.
4. CONSEQUENCE
The recall of Governor William Bligh, the permanent dismantling and replacement of the NSW Corps by Governor Lachlan Macquarie s 73rd Regiment, and the formal transition from penal fiefdom to civil rule of law.

EP06 // 10 TALL FACTS & 10 LYRICAL LIFELINES

10 Tactical Tall Facts

  1. Over 300 convicts, predominantly Irish political transportees from the 1798 uprising, broke out of the Government Agricultural Farm at Castle Hill on March 4, 1804.
  2. Major George Johnston rode through the night from Sydney to Parramatta, covering 24 km in under 4 hours to mobilize troops and loyalist civilians.
  3. Philip Cunningham approached under a flag of truce at Rouse Hill; he was unceremoniously seized and summarily hanged at Hawkesbury without a court trial.
  4. The 1804 clash marked the first declaration of martial law in Australian history, enacted across Parramatta, Castle Hill, and the Hawkesbury basin.
  5. The New South Wales Corps (nicknamed Rum Corps) controlled the import of spirits, inflating prices up to 400% as a captive barter currency.
  6. John Macarthur arrived with £497 in capital and used military access to accumulate over 10,000 acres at the Cowpastures (Camden) for merino breeding.
  7. Governor William Bligh was arrested on January 26, 1808—the 20th anniversary of the First Fleet landing—when 400 soldiers of the NSW Corps marched on Government House.
  8. The Corps rebel administration governed New South Wales illegally for nearly two years until the arrival of Lachlan Macquarie on December 28, 1809.
  9. Major George Johnston was subsequently court-martialed at Chelsea Hospital in London in 1811 and cashiered (dismissed) from the British Army.
  10. The 1808 rebellion forced the British Colonial Office to abandon military administration and assign regular British regiments to enforce statutory oversight.
